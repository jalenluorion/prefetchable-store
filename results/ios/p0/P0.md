# P0 of the iOS port: what limits each phone number (2026-11-22)

iPhone 15 Plus (iPhone15,5, A16: 2 performance + 4 efficiency cores, 5.94 GB, iOS 27.2 build 24B119), plugged in and
charging (battery 80-90 %), low power off; the app `com.example.phonestore` with the increased-memory-limit entitlement
(os_proc_available_memory 4.27 GB at launch). Mac: M4 16 GB, macOS 26.5.1, on AC. No model ran on either machine:
kernel microbenches, the file-cache test on the store's bytes, and a Python cache simulation of recorded routing.
Every limit below is named **hardware** (the device's rate or budget) or **implementation** (our code or the OS's
policy, movable).

## The two go / no-go answers

**File cache as expert cache: GO: 2.1-2.6 GB of extra expert cache outside the app's memory budget.** The OS keeps **2.1-2.6 GB** of clean,
file-backed store pages resident for the foreground app (plateau of a sweep over the 15.7 GB mapped store: 2.56 GB
max, 2.1-2.4 GB steady), **with phys_footprint and os_proc_available_memory unchanged** (0.03 GB / 4.26 GB before and
after), **no memory warning, no kill**, and the same plateau with 1.5 GB of dirty memory held (2.42 GB max; footprint
1.53, available 2.76). So it is **on top of** the app's 4.27 GB budget. Costs, measured per 1.77 MB record: a resident
record read in place through the mapping costs 0.05-0.07 ms of user time and zero kernel time (25-39 GB/s on one
thread, 35-39 GB/s on 8); the same record copied by buffered pread costs 0.13-0.20 ms of kernel time (9-13 GB/s). Cold, the mapping is slower
than today's reads: a page fault per 16 KB page (108 faults and 108 page-ins per record, no clustering), 0.40-0.45 GB/s on
one thread and 0.34-0.55 GB/s on 8 threads, against 0.69-0.76 GB/s for pread (F_NOCACHE or buffered) on 8 threads in
the same session (0.78 GB/s in the earlier drive test). madvise(MADV_WILLNEED) is synchronous on iOS (the call itself
takes the record's read time, 2.7-3 ms) and gives no extra rate with 1-16 records in flight (0.50-0.64 GB/s). Background
30 s and back: half stays (2.11 -> 1.04 GB), the process survives. Design consequence: misses are read with buffered pread
(0.69-0.76 GB/s, and it fills the same cache the mapping sees), hits are taken in place from the mapping (no slot
copy); the OS tier either adds to today's 1.27 GB dirty cache (3.3 GB in all) or replaces it, so the dirty budget can
hold the Mac's 8-bit resident weights for identical tokens (see the tier table). Interference with a CPU kernel load (section 2.4): -6 to -8 % of the kernel's rate while pread reads cold records at
0.7 GB/s, -14 to -18 % through page faults; 1 GB/s of resident records costs -4 to -5 %, with zero kernel time in place
and half a core of kernel copy through pread.

**Exact Metal as the phone's main path: NO-GO for P1 (CPU first), Metal kept as an exact side path.** The exact Metal
kernels are bit-identical to the CPU kernels on the A16 (0 mismatching outputs of 6 checks: 4-bit gate/up, 4-bit down,
8-bit router, 8-bit / 4-bit out_proj matvec, the batched 8-bit group dot at N = 64). Estimated compute per token from
their rates: **Metal 67 ms (4-bit resident) / 78 ms (8-bit resident) vs CPU 55 / 73 ms** at burst (the Metal cells ran
with the phone already at thermal "serious", the CPU's best cells cool): no ceiling raised.
Under sustained load the CPU experts' kernel falls from 32-38 to 19-20 GB/s within 4 s (power limit, thermal "fair"),
the GPU holds 23-25 GB/s; CPU and GPU together give 26-28 GB/s, the same as either alone (one shared power budget,
**hardware**). A command buffer round trip costs 0.42 ms on the A16 (0.15 ms on the M4): a decode that syncs CPU and
GPU once per layer pays 17 ms per token in round trips alone. Batched prefill: the GPU's group dot runs 50-71 GMAC/s
vs the CPU's 120 GMAC/s (6 threads). Where Metal does win: the large 8-bit resident matrices stream at 44-48 GB/s on
the GPU even at thermal "serious" (at or above our simple GPU read kernel's 43 GB/s: **hardware**), so a pipelined GPU encoding of the dense
part (no per-layer round trip) is the one Metal design worth a P2 test.

## 2. File cache as the expert cache (phone)

`-fileCacheTest grow|dirty|paths|bg` (phone-store `native/Sources/DMBench/filecache.mm`). The 40 store files on the
phone (`Documents/model/store4/layer_XX.psc`, the compressed store, 15.67 GB; used as bytes) mapped whole, PROT_READ,
**MAP_SHARED** (clean file-backed pages, never written: MAP_PRIVATE would behave the same for reads; MAP_SHARED rules
out any copy-on-write accounting). All 40 files (15.67 GB of address space) mapped without the extended-virtual-
addressing entitlement. Records = 1,769,472-byte ranges at multiples of the record size (108 pages of 16 KB, page
aligned). Every second: mincore over all 15.67 GB, phys_footprint, os_proc_available_memory, memory warnings
(UIApplication notification + a DISPATCH_SOURCE_TYPE_MEMORYPRESSURE source), thermal, faults and page-ins
(task_events_info), touch rates split cold / resident by mincore just before each touch.

Answer: on the phone the read paths cost the compute little at the rates a decode needs. Reading cold records at
the drive's rate takes 6-8 % of the kernel's rate through pread (a or b) and 14-18 % through page faults (c, d: one
fault and one page-in per 16 KB page, 3,000x the faults of pread). Taking 1 GB/s of resident records costs 4-5 % either

drops in the unpaced rows are memory bandwidth (the readers run at 8-22 GB/s against a 51 GB/s bus), not the paths'
kernel work. No sign of reclaim cost: footprint 0.31 GB and available 3.98 GB steady, no warnings; the hot set
shrinks only when cold reads push it out (0.80 -> 0.10 GB during the c / d windows, refilled before each resident
window). The M4's doubling of compute with the buffered cache on (a reviewer's note) does not appear here at
these rates; the difference to check on the Mac is the read rate (the Mac's 3.3 GB cache took far more GB/s of
copies) and the cache growth (the phone's cache plateaus at 2.1-2.6 GB without footprint).

### 2.5 Kernels on file-backed pointers; what store.h needs

The kernels take plain pointers (`mv_aff4(codes, scales, biases, K, r0, r1, x, y)`) and load with vld1q (no alignment
requirement); in a record, codes start at offset 0 and the fp16 scales / biases at even offsets (589824 + ...), so a
pointer into the mapping at `e * 1,769,472` is usable as is (it is 16 KB aligned). The bench's c / d paths and the
re-touch phases are exactly that read. What store.h would need for "slot = view of file pages": (1) `Cache::rec(L,
slot)` returns `map_base[L] + e * rec` for a held expert instead of `pool(L).row(slot)`; (2) "held" = the engine's
belief (it cannot pin OS pages; mincore per record costs ~10 us and is a hint only), so a held record can fault: the
compute path must tolerate a fault (it does: a fault is a stall, never wrong bytes); (3) the reader threads become
buffered pread into a 1.77 MB scratch per reader (fills the OS cache at 0.64-0.73 GB/s; F_NOCACHE does not) or a
prefault touch (0.45-0.51 GB/s); (4) eviction belongs to the OS (LRU-like, tier_sim_lru.py) unless a dirty tier
stays in front; (5) a Mem mode (`Mem::FILE`) and its byte budget outside the app's footprint.

(the M4 stage split's 3.1 + 0.5 ms, scaled by the matvec ratio). **Calibration: on the Mac's own bench rates it predicts
32.5 ms/token against the measured 31.5-33.6 (factor 1.00)**, so the phone figure is a ratio of measured rates, not a
guess. Bytes per token: 8-bit resident 2.06 GB + experts 0.57 GB; 4-bit resident 1.10 + 0.57 GB.

(~45 positions/s compute ceiling, burst) vs ~7.4 ms on the M4. The prefill's other limit is the drive: a long prompt
touches every expert of every layer, 18.1 GB of records = 23 s at 0.78 GB/s (**hardware**), whatever the compute.

## 5. What the core must gain (requests, by file)

1. `src/core/store.cpp`, `include/dmeng/store.h`: **a file-backed tier** (`Mem::FILE`): slots as views of the mapped
   store (`rec()` returns `map_base + e * rec`), misses read with buffered pread (fills the OS cache), the OS cache
   counted outside the app budget; optionally a dirty tier in front. Measured gain on the 15 Plus: 2.1-2.6 GB of extra
   cache, 265 -> 134 MB/token.
2. `src/core/store.cpp` policy: **re-tune for 0.8-2.5 GB** (half-life, or per-layer caps / hazard as the phone's MLX
   engine): at 1.27 GB the decayed count reads 265 MB/token, LRU 254, the phone's own engine 194, Belady 148.
3. `src/core/pool.cpp`, `include/dmeng/pool.h`: **a QoS option and core-count awareness** (2 P + 4 E): 4 threads is
   the best count for 8-bit, 6 for 4-bit; background QoS is 3-4x slower.
4. `src/core/kernels.cpp`: **the 4-bit matvec is compute-bound on the A16** (57 GMAC/s, 0.6 of the bandwidth): a
   faster 4-bit inner loop (e.g. pre-shifted nibble planes, I8 activations where exactness allows) moves the experts
   and the 4-bit resident; the 8-bit one is at the bandwidth already.
5. `src/models/qwen3_5_moe`: **decode must name records early on the phone** (the drive is the ceiling: named early
   vs cache only is 3.0 vs 2.4-2.6 tok/s today, 6.0 vs 3.9-4.5 with the file cache).
6. `bindings/c`: a prefill progress hook in `LM::prefill` and `dm_set_cache_bytes` (resize under memory warnings).

## 6. Not measured, and why

- Other phones' drive rates and memory budgets (8 / 12 GB tiers are labelled assumptions); the A17 Pro / A18 have
  different CPUs and GPUs (compute taken as the A16's).
- The tier table uses pooled policies on the common store's record size; the phone's .psc records are compressed
  (15.7 vs 18.1 GB), so a compressed-store engine would read ~13 % fewer bytes per miss, but its tokens differ from
  the Mac's (separate quantisation); identical tokens need the common store on the phone (not copied in P0).
- A sustained CPU run on the 8-bit and 4-bit resident shapes (only the experts' kernel ran 60 s); the 0.62 factor is
  applied to all classes.
- A decode-shaped Metal encoding (all layers of a token in one command buffer) was not built; only per-matrix
  dispatches were timed.
- Whether file pages survive longer under other apps' pressure (only 30 s with Settings in front).
